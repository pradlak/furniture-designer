Concealed hinges — where to drill the holes in the doors

Updated: 26 November 2026

The concealed (cup) hinge is the standard in board furniture: the cup sits in a hole drilled in the back of the door, and the arm is screwed to a mounting plate on the side of the cabinet. A badly drilled hole means a door hanging crooked or a hole right through — below are the typical dimensions and the order of work.

Cup hole dimensions — at a glance

DimensionTypical valueNotes
Cup diameter35 mmthe most common; smaller ones (26 mm) exist for narrow and thin fronts
Hole depthapprox. 11–13 mmwith 18 mm board, 5–7 mm of material remains
From the door edge to the edge of the hole3–6 mmi.e. the cup centre approx. 20.5–23.5 mm from the edge
From the top / bottom of the door to the cup centreapprox. 70–100 mmso that the hinge doesn't hit a top/bottom panel or a shelf
Holes for the cup screws / dowelsdepends on the modele.g. 45–48 mm spacing — see the hinge data sheet

Always check the data sheet. Manufacturers (Blum, Hettich, GTV, Häfele and others) give the exact edge distance, depth and overlay for each hinge. The values in the table are typical and work well with most hinges, but a specific model may differ by a millimetre or two.

The edge distance decides the overlay

The distance from the edge of the cup to the edge of the door (often marked „K” or „C” in catalogues), together with the height of the mounting plate, sets how much the door overlaps the side of the cabinet. The further from the edge you drill, the less the door overlaps the side. That's why you should drill all the doors in one unit at the same distance — otherwise the gaps between the fronts will be uneven.

Small differences can be corrected after hanging the door: most hinges can be adjusted sideways, vertically and in depth (with screws on the arm and the mounting plate), usually within about ±2 mm.

Types of concealed hinges

By the position of the door

  • Full overlay (straight) — the door covers the front edge of the cabinet side. The most common in wardrobes and kitchen units.
  • Half overlay — when two doors hang on one side (e.g. a partition between two cabinets without a break) and each covers half of its thickness.
  • Inset — the door sits between the sides of the cabinet, flush with its front.

The cup in the door is drilled the same way for all three — what differs is the crank of the hinge arm.

By the opening angle

  • 110° — the standard, enough for most cabinets.
  • 155–170° — wide-opening doors, useful e.g. when there are internal drawers or pull-out baskets inside. These are often zero-protrusion hinges — once open, the door doesn't intrude into the cabinet opening.
  • Angled hinges (e.g. for corner units, angled fronts) — a separate group with its own dimensions.

Soft-close or not

A soft-close hinge has a damper built into the cup or arm — the door pulls itself shut and doesn't slam. You drill the hole the same way. Without soft-close the hinge is cheaper, and for doors with a „push to open” system you usually take hinges without a spring.

Mounting plate

The mounting plate is screwed to the side of the cabinet — usually at the same height as the centre of the cup, about 37 mm from the front edge of the side (the so-called 32 mm system line). Mounting plates come in different heights (e.g. 0, 2, 3 mm) and that also changes the door overlay — choose it from the manufacturer's table.

How many hinges per door and where to put them

As a guide, for 18 mm board doors up to about 60 cm wide:

  • up to about 90 cm high — 2 hinges,
  • up to about 160 cm — 3 hinges,
  • up to about 200 cm — 4 hinges,
  • taller — 5 hinges.

Heavy fronts (lacquered MDF, mirrored doors, fronts wider than 60 cm) need one more hinge. Doors wider than about 60 cm are better replaced by double doors altogether — a single door drops over time.

Put the outer hinges about 70–100 mm from the top and bottom edges of the door, and the rest evenly in between. While doing so, check that the mounting plate on the side doesn't hit a top/bottom panel, a fixed shelf or the runner of an internal drawer — if it does, move the hinge a few centimetres.

How to drill the hole, step by step

  1. Mark the side and the heights. Lay the door face down. Mark the hinge edge and the heights of the cup centres measured from the bottom edge.
  2. Find the centre. Measure the cup centre distance from the edge (e.g. 22.5 mm = 5 mm to the edge of the hole + 17.5 mm radius). A hinge jig or a try square helps a lot here.
  3. Set the depth stop. A 35 mm Forstner bit in a pillar drill or in a drill guide with a depth stop. Do a test on an offcut of the same board.
  4. Drill at low speed, without pressing hard, pulling the bit out every so often to clear the chips.
  5. Press or screw in the cup, set the hinge square to the edge and screw it down. Screw the mounting plates to the side, hang the door and adjust the gaps.

Easier: order the fronts drilled. Most panel-cutting shops and MDF front makers drill cup holes for a few złoty (Polish currency) per hole. Just tell them the hinge side and the heights of the centres from the bottom — they set the edge distance for a standard hinge.

Frequently asked questions

What drill bit do you use for a concealed hinge hole?

A 35 mm Forstner (hinge-boring) bit, ideally in a pillar drill or with a guide or jig that limits the depth. An ordinary hand-held drill can easily go right through the front.

How deep should the hinge cup hole be?

Typically 11–13 mm — the cup of a standard hinge is about 11.5 mm deep. With 18 mm board a few millimetres of material remain at the front. The exact depth is given in the hinge data sheet.

How many hinges should a door have?

As a guide: up to about 90 cm high 2 hinges, up to about 160 cm — 3, up to about 200 cm — 4, taller — 5. For heavy or wide doors (e.g. with a mirror) add one more hinge.

What is the difference between a full overlay and an inset hinge?

A full overlay (straight) hinge gives a door that covers the side of the cabinet, a half overlay one — two doors on one side, and an inset hinge — a door hidden between the sides of the cabinet. The cup in the door is drilled the same way; the hinge arm differs.
