Edge banding — how to work it out and which edges to band

Updated: 26 November 2026

After cutting, chipboard has a raw edge with visible chips. It is covered with a tape — edge banding. When you order panel cutting you need to know three things: which edges to band, with what thickness, and how much that reduces the size of the cut piece. Here it all is, step by step, with an example.

Types of edge banding and which thickness to choose

At panel-cutting shops you'll most often come across ABS banding (plastic) in several thicknesses and thin melamine (paper) banding. The thickness of the banding matters for two reasons: it affects how durable the edge is and the cutting size of the piece.

BandingWhere it works well
Melamine (approx. 0.4 mm)Edges that are barely visible and don't get knocked; the cheapest, easy to apply with an iron at home.
ABS 0.8 mmThe most common choice for cabinet edges: sides, top/bottom panels, shelves.
ABS 1 mmEdges that get knocked more often — e.g. the front of the bottom panel, shelves in cabinets used every day.
ABS 2 mmFronts (doors, drawers) and the most exposed edges; after banding the edge is usually rounded.

Lacquered MDF fronts have no banding — the edge is routed and lacquered together with the face. HDF backs usually aren't banded either.

In the designer you set the banding thickness on the material (the Materials window, the Edge banding field): none, 0.4, 0.8, 1 or 2 mm. Chipboard has 0.8 mm by default, lacquered MDF and HDF — no banding. If you want fronts with thicker banding than the cabinet, add a separate material for them (the same decor, 2 mm banding).

Which edges to band and which not

The rule is simple: you band what can be seen. Edges hidden against a wall, under a worktop, at the back or butted against another board don't need tape — you save banding and the cost of banding.

  • Cabinet sides — always the front; the top when the side is taller than the neighbouring furniture and its top can be seen; not the back if the cabinet has a back panel.
  • Top/bottom panels — yes at the front (you see it when the doors are open); not at the back by the back panel; the side edges are usually hidden between the sides.
  • Shelves — only the front. The shelf ends touch the cabinet sides, and the back is against the back panel.
  • Fronts — all four edges, because doors and drawers are seen from every side, especially when open.
  • Underside edges — usually no banding, because they can't be seen.
  • Fillers and wall panels — the edge hidden against the wall: no banding; the face edge visible from the front: yes.

How the program does it. By default the designer bands only visible edges: it checks whether there is another board, a filler, a worktop, an obstacle or a wall right next to the edge. It skips underside edges, edges covered by a worktop, and in cabinets with a back panel — the back edges of the sides and top/bottom panels. Shelves get banding only at the front, fronts on all four edges. You can change the edges by hand in the cabinet card, in the Parts section (the „edge banding” fields); the ↺ button restores the automatic choice.

Cutting size: finished size minus the banding

Banding has a thickness, so after banding the piece „grows”. For the finished part to have exactly the designed size, the board is cut smaller: from each dimension you subtract the banding thickness as many times as there are banded edges on that dimension.

  • The length is reduced by the short edges (the ones at the ends of the length).
  • The width is reduced by the long edges (the ones along the length).

Example 1 — a wardrobe side

The side has a finished size of 2390 × 580 mm, 0.8 mm banding only at the front (a long edge). The length doesn't change, the width is reduced by one banding: 580 − 0.8 = 579.2 mm. Cutting size: 2390 × 579.2 mm.

Example 2 — a drawer front

A 596 × 237 mm front, 2 mm banding on all four edges. Length: 596 − 2 × 2 = 592 mm, width: 237 − 2 × 2 = 233 mm. Cutting size: 592 × 233 mm.

The program calculates it in exactly the same way — in the cabinet card you can see the „cutting” size for each part, and the cut list has two columns: the finished size and the cutting size.

Watch out when ordering. Not every cutting shop wants the same size. Some accept the cutting size, others the finished size and subtract the banding themselves. Ask before placing the order — otherwise the parts will come out 1–4 mm too small or too large. The program saves the file for panel cutting at meble.pl (a Polish online board shop) with finished sizes, because that's what their configurator accepts.

How many metres of edge banding you need

Edge banding is bought or charged in running metres. You work them out from the cut list: for each row, multiply the length of each banded edge by the number of pieces and add everything up separately for each thickness and colour.

The cabinets (sides, top/bottom panels, shelves) come to 14.34 + 4.58 + 6.86 = 25.78 m of 0.8 mm banding. The doors take 32.40 m of 2 mm banding. As you can see, the fronts can use more banding than the whole cabinet.

Allowance for trimming

The tape is applied with an overhang and trimmed flush with the board, so a little is wasted on every edge. If you band it yourself, add a few centimetres per edge or about 10% to the total. When the cutting shop does the banding, the allowance is usually included in the service — you pay for the metres of edge.

Edge banding notation on the cut list

In the Excel file from the cut list each row has the columns:

  • Banding along the length [pcs] — how many long edges to band (0, 1 or 2),
  • Banding along the width [pcs] — how many short edges to band (0, 1 or 2),
  • Banding thickness [mm],
  • Banded edges — in words: front, back, top, bottom, left, right.

In files for cutting services (e.g. meble.pl, PRO100) you'll also see a shorthand: „=” — band both edges of that dimension, „-” — one, an empty field — no banding. With one edge the cutting shop doesn't know which of the two to choose, so for asymmetric parts it's worth adding a note or attaching the description from Excel.

Frequently asked questions

What thickness of edge banding should I choose for furniture?

Most often 0.8 mm ABS banding for the edges of cabinets and shelves, and 1–2 mm for fronts and edges that get knocked. Thin melamine banding works on edges that are barely visible.

Do you need to subtract the banding thickness from the size of the piece?

Yes, if you order the cutting size: from the finished size you subtract the banding thickness for each banded edge of that dimension. Some cutting shops, however, accept the finished size and subtract the banding themselves — ask which size to give.

How many metres of edge banding do I need for a wardrobe?

Add up the lengths of all the banded edges from the cut list and add an allowance for trimming the ends — usually a few centimetres per edge or about 10% of the total.

Which board edges don't need banding?

Edges that can't be seen usually aren't banded: back edges at the back panel, underside edges, edges against a wall or covered by a worktop or another board. Shelves are usually banded only at the front.

Check it on an example: open „Alcove wardrobe”, click a cabinet and expand Parts — for each board you'll see the banded edges and the cutting size. Then Cut list… saves the whole list with the banding to Excel.
