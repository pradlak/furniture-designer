Free furniture design software — a comparison

Updated: 26 November 2026

Want to make a wardrobe, a built-in unit or kitchen cabinets yourself and looking for a program that costs nothing? We compared the most popular tools on what really matters for DIY furniture: whether they are free, whether they run in the browser, and whether you get a list of boards to cut at the end.

In fairness: this comparison was prepared by the team behind Meble Samemu. We checked the information about the other programs on their official websites as of November 2026 — prices and licence terms may change, so look at the maker's website before you decide.

What to look for when choosing furniture software

Any 3D program will do for a „nice picture”. For furniture you make yourself, three things matter more:

  • A cut list. The board supplier or panel-cutting shop needs a list of boards: size, quantity, edge banding on the edges. If the program doesn't give you one, you have to work it out by hand — and that's where mistakes happen most easily.
  • Thinking in cabinets. A furniture program knows that a cabinet is made of sides, top and bottom panels, a back and shelves, and works out their sizes itself. In a general 3D program you draw each board separately.
  • Barrier to entry. Do you need to install a program, create an account, spend several evenings learning the tools — or do you have a design after a quarter of an hour?

Meble Samemu

Meble Samemu is a free designer in the browser, made precisely for furniture you build yourself from cut boards. You enter the dimensions of an alcove or a room (including an attic with a sloped ceiling), add cabinets, shelves, doors, drawers, fillers and a worktop, and the program works out all the boards itself. Finally you download a cut list with cutting sizes, edge banding and accessories — in Excel, CSV or as a file for panel cutting at meble.pl (a Polish online board shop). You don't need to create an account; signing in with Google is just an option for keeping projects in the cloud.

What Meble Samemu doesn't have (as of November 2026):

  • sliding doors — it designs hinged doors and drawers; you can design the cabinets for a sliding-door wardrobe, but the doors themselves have to be ordered separately,
  • photorealistic renders — the 3D view is for designing, not for „photos” of the interior,
  • cutting optimisation on sheets and files for CNC machines (e.g. DXF) — laying the pieces out on the boards is done by the supplier or the cutting configurator,
  • pricing — the program doesn't know the board prices at your supplier,
  • comfort on a phone — it works in the browser, but a computer with a mouse is best for designing.

SketchUp Free

SketchUp Free is the free version of SketchUp that runs in the browser. It requires an account (Trimble ID) and according to the maker it is intended for non-commercial use only — enough for your own wardrobe at home. It's a general-purpose 3D modelling program: it gives you a lot of freedom, but you draw each board yourself, keep track of the thicknesses and sizes yourself and put together the list of boards to cut yourself. A good choice if you like drawing and want to design something unusual; to start with you need to spend some time learning it.

PRO100

PRO100 is a Polish program made by Ecru, popular for years among joiners and furniture companies. It is paid, runs on Windows, and the maker offers a demo version to try. It has rich libraries and tools for professionals — for a single wardrobe at home that's usually more than you need.

Polyboard

Polyboard (Wood Designer) is a program for designing cabinet furniture, installed on your computer. According to the maker, the free version lets you design a piece of furniture, but the production documents — plans, cutting lists, CNC files — are in the paid version. The maker also offers a service that prepares a cutting list from a design made in the free version. A powerful program, aimed at workshops and production.

IKEA planners (PAX, METOD)

IKEA has free planners in the browser, e.g. for PAX wardrobes and METOD kitchens. They are convenient and need no installation, but you design with ready-made IKEA parts — in standard sizes. At the end you get a list of products to buy, not a list of boards to cut. If the alcove has an unusual size or a slope, you have to improvise with fillers and trimming.

FreeCAD and Blender

FreeCAD is a free, open-source CAD program for parametric modelling, and Blender — a free, open-source 3D graphics suite (modelling, rendering, animation). You install both on Windows, macOS or Linux and both are very capable — but they aren't furniture programs. You build cabinets from individual solids and prepare the list of boards yourself (or look for add-ons). Blender is great for photorealistic visualisations; FreeCAD — for precise technical drawings. For a beginner both have a high barrier to entry.

What about the cutting configurators of board suppliers?

Many board suppliers and shops have forms where you enter the cut pieces and order the cutting. These aren't design programs — they assume you already have the list of boards. That's why they combine well with a designer: you design the furniture, download the cut list and paste it in or load it from a file. We describe how to do this with meble.pl as an example in the guide panel cutting at meble.pl from a CSV file.

Which program should you choose?

  • An alcove wardrobe, attic storage, kitchen cabinets from boards cut to size — Meble Samemu: a design in the browser and a ready cut list for ordering panel cutting.
  • An unusual piece or a decorative detail you want to draw from scratch — SketchUp Free (for private use) or FreeCAD.
  • A wardrobe or kitchen from ready-made IKEA modules — the IKEA planners.
  • A company or joinery workshop, production, CNC — professional programs, e.g. PRO100 or paid Polyboard.
  • A photorealistic interior visualisation — Blender (or SketchUp with add-ons).

Nothing stops you from combining tools: for example, designing the cabinets and downloading the cut list in Meble Samemu, and doing a visualisation of the whole room in another program.

Frequently asked questions

Which free furniture design program gives you a cut list?

Meble Samemu gives you a cut list for free (Excel, CSV and a file for panel cutting at meble.pl). The free version of Polyboard lets you design, but production documents, including cutting lists, are in the paid version. In SketchUp Free, FreeCAD and Blender you have to prepare the list of boards yourself.

Is SketchUp Free any good for designing furniture?

Yes, for 3D modelling — it runs in the browser and is free for non-commercial use. However, you draw each board yourself and work out the cutting sizes yourself.

Is PRO100 free?

No. PRO100 is a paid Windows program made by Ecru; the maker offers a demo version.

Do you need to install a program to design furniture?

Not always. Meble Samemu, SketchUp Free and the IKEA planners run in the browser. PRO100, Polyboard, FreeCAD and Blender have to be installed.

Try it in 2 minutes: open the ready-made example „Alcove wardrobe” or „Kitchen units”, change the dimensions and click Cut list… — you'll see the list of boards to cut.
