How much board do you need for a wardrobe? Worked out step by step

Updated: 26 November 2026

Before you order panel cutting, it's worth knowing how many sheets of board the wardrobe will take — so you can compare offers from board suppliers and not get a surprise at the till. Below is a simple method: from the cut list, through square metres, to the number of sheets with an allowance for waste. At the end — whether to choose 18 mm or 16 mm board.

1. Start from the cut list, not from the wardrobe dimensions

The wardrobe dimensions (e.g. 240 × 250 × 65 cm) don't tell you how much board you'll use — inside there are sides, top and bottom panels, shelves, and at the front doors and fillers. That's why you first need a cut list: all the rectangles to be cut, with their dimensions and quantities.

In the designer you get such a list with the 💾 Cut list… button — e.g. in Excel. The program works out the sides, top/bottom panels, shelves, backs, fronts and fillers itself, and merges identical pieces into one row with a quantity. The list is split by material, so you can see straight away what is chipboard, what is HDF and what is e.g. MDF. More about the list itself: cut lists and panel cutting.

2. Work out the square metres

For each row of the list: length × width × quantity. You convert millimetres to metres (divide by 1000), so e.g. a 2390 × 600 mm side is 2.39 × 0.6 = 1.434 m². In Excel just add a column with a formula and sum it separately for each material.

Count each material and each thickness separately. 18 mm white board, wood-colour board for the fronts, 3 mm HDF for the backs and a worktop are four different purchases — each with its own sheets and its own waste.

3. Example: a 240 cm alcove wardrobe with three cabinets

Take a wardrobe similar to the „Alcove wardrobe” example: a 2400 × 2500 mm alcove, three 780 × 2390 × 600 mm cabinets on legs, 11 shelves, double doors on each cabinet, side fillers, a top filler and a plinth. All in 18 mm board. The dimensions below are rounded — the program gives the exact ones.

4. From square metres to sheets

Chipboard is sold in sheets. A common size is 2800 × 2070 mm, i.e. 2.8 × 2.07 = 5.796 m² — but sizes vary by manufacturer and supplier, so check the sheet size where you order.

  • Without waste: 22.56 ÷ 5.796 ≈ 3.9 sheets.
  • With 15% waste: 22.56 × 1.15 ≈ 25.9 m² → ≈ 4.5 sheets.
  • With 20% waste: 22.56 × 1.2 ≈ 27.1 m² → ≈ 4.7 sheets.

Conclusion: for this wardrobe you should count on 5 sheets of 18 mm board, not 4 as the area alone would suggest.

5. Where cutting waste comes from

Cut pieces never fit onto a sheet perfectly. Waste arises because of:

  • The saw kerf — every cut eats up a few millimetres of board.
  • Sheet edges — the edges are often chipped, so cutting frequently starts by trimming them straight.
  • Shapes that don't fit — from a 2070 mm wide sheet you can cut three 600 mm sides (1800 mm), leaving a 270 mm strip that is good for narrow fillers at most.
  • Grain — with a wood-pattern board the pieces can't be rotated freely, so that the grain runs in one direction. This usually increases waste compared with a plain board.

For a first estimate allow 15–20% extra. The exact number of sheets is given by cutting optimisation software or by the supplier — after laying the pieces out on the sheets. 6. HDF backs — separately

3 mm HDF backs are a separate material, so you count them separately. In our example these are three boards of roughly 780 × 2390 mm, together about 5.59 m². An HDF sheet of the same size has 5.8 m², so „on paper” one is enough — but across the 2070 mm width only two 780 mm strips fit (1560 mm), and the third back needs a second sheet. That's why you should always check that the pieces physically fit on the sheet, not just the area.

18 mm or 16 mm board?

This question comes up with every wardrobe. Both thicknesses are popular, but they suit different jobs.

18 mm board

  • Stiffer — shelves sag less, the cabinet is more stable.
  • The most common choice for wardrobe cabinets and shelves, bookcases and kitchen units.
  • A wider choice of decors and edge banding at suppliers.
  • Heavier — tall wardrobe sides in 18 mm board need two people when fitting.

16 mm board

  • Lighter and usually a little cheaper.
  • Good for smaller furniture: chests of drawers, bedside cabinets, narrow bookcases, and also for drawer sides and backs.
  • Shelves made of 16 mm board sag more noticeably — make them narrower or add a stiffener under the shelf.
  • Less room for screws and confirmats in the edge, so you have to drill more precisely.

Rule of thumb: a floor-to-ceiling wardrobe with shelves for clothes or books — 18 mm board. A small chest of drawers or furniture you move around often — 16 mm is enough. The back in both cases is 3 mm HDF.

In the designer, thickness is a property of the material. Change it in the 🎨 Materials window, and the sizes of the top/bottom panels, shelves and fronts on the cut list are recalculated automatically — e.g. a top panel in a 780 mm cabinet made of 16 mm board is 780 − 2 × 16 = 748 mm.

You can also combine both thicknesses: a cabinet in 18 mm board and shelves in 16 mm. In the cabinet card, in the Shelves section, choose the 16 mm board in the Shelf material field (in a new project it's already on the list; if it isn't, choose + New material… and set the thickness to 16). The shelves will be in a separate group on the cut list, and in the meble.pl export — in that board's file.

Frequently asked questions

How many sheets of board do you need for a 240 cm floor-to-ceiling wardrobe?

A typical 240 × 250 cm alcove wardrobe with three cabinets, shelves, doors and fillers takes about 22–23 m² of 18 mm board. A 2800 × 2070 mm sheet has 5.8 m², so after adding the waste it usually comes to 5 sheets. HDF backs are counted separately.

How much should you add for waste when cutting boards?

For an estimate allow about 15–20% more than the total area of the cut pieces. The exact number of sheets is given by cutting optimisation software or by the supplier after laying the pieces out on the sheets.

18 mm or 16 mm board for a wardrobe?

For wardrobe cabinets and shelves, 18 mm board is the usual choice — it is stiffer and the shelves sag less. 16 mm board is lighter and usually a little cheaper; it suits smaller furniture, narrow shelves and parts that don't carry much weight.

Are HDF backs counted together with the chipboard?

No. HDF is a separate material, bought in separate sheets, so its area and number of sheets are worked out separately.
