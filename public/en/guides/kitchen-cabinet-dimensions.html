Standard kitchen cabinet dimensions

Updated: 26 November 2026

A kitchen is designed around dimensions that most furniture and appliance manufacturers use in a similar way. Below we have gathered typical values for base units, wall units, tall units, the worktop and the plinth — as a starting point for your own design. For built-in appliances always check the installation instructions of the model you are buying as well.

Base units

A base unit is a cabinet standing on legs, covered with a worktop. The most common cabinet height is 720 mm. Add the legs (usually 100–150 mm) and the worktop (28–40 mm), and the worktop ends up about 90 cm above the floor.

The cabinet depth is usually 510–560 mm. A standard worktop is 600 mm deep, so it sticks out a few centimetres in front of the fronts — water and crumbs then fall onto the floor rather than onto the doors. There is also a little room behind the cabinets for pipes and sockets.

How to choose a worktop height that suits you

A comfortable worktop is roughly 10–15 cm below your elbow. Tall people are better suited by a 92–95 cm worktop, shorter people by 85–88 cm. The easiest way to change the height is with the legs or the plinth, without altering the cabinets.

Cabinet widths — modules

Kitchen furniture manufacturers make cabinets in widths that go up in steps of 50 or 100 mm. You will most often see 300, 400, 450, 500, 600 and 800 mm, as well as narrow pull-out larders of 150–200 mm and corner units of 900–1100 mm. When making furniture yourself you don't have to stick to these, but it's worth it, because:

  • built-in appliances (dishwasher, oven, sink) are designed for 45 and 60 cm cabinets,
  • ready-made baskets, pull-outs and cutlery inserts fit the typical widths,
  • cabinets wider than about 90–100 cm have sagging shelves and very wide doors.

Doors wider than about 60 cm are better made as double doors. In a sink unit the shelf and the back panel are usually left out where the pipes go, or a hole is cut in them.

Drawers

Drawers in base units are handier than shelves — you can see everything from above. A typical layout is 3 equal drawers or 1 shallow + 2 deep (e.g. for cutlery and pans). Runners are chosen to match the cabinet depth: with a 510–560 mm cabinet, 450–500 mm runners usually fit. The length and side clearance are given by the runner manufacturer — stick to their table.

Wall units

Wall units are shallower — usually 300–350 mm, so they don't overshadow the worktop and you don't bang your head on them. The height is chosen to suit the room: 720 mm is the classic, 900 mm with high ceilings, and 360–580 mm above the fridge or the cooker hood.

Between the worktop and the underside of the wall units you usually leave 50–60 cm. The underside of the units is then about 140–150 cm above the floor. A smaller gap makes working at the worktop harder (and standing e.g. a coffee machine on it), a larger one makes the top shelves harder to reach.

Above the hob the distance to the cooker hood is set by the hood manufacturer in the instructions — it is usually larger above a gas hob than above an induction hob. The units next to the hood can be hung lower or at the same height as the underside of the hood.

Tall units

A tall unit is a floor-standing cabinet — for an oven, a built-in fridge or a pull-out larder. It is usually 2000–2200 mm high (with legs) and as deep as the base units. It often reaches the line of the top edge of the wall units, and the rest up to the ceiling is filled with a filler or an extra cabinet.

Plinth

The plinth (bottom filler) hides the legs. Its height is the height of the legs, i.e. usually 100–150 mm. It is set a few centimetres back from the fronts, so there is room for your feet when you stand at the worktop. The plinth is usually fixed to the legs with clips, so it is easy to take off, e.g. to replace the dishwasher.

Space for built-in appliances

The dimensions below are approximate. Every model has its own installation instructions with the exact recess — download them from the manufacturer's website before you order the boards.

  • Dishwasher — a 600 mm wide recess (slimline dishwashers: 450 mm) under the worktop, with no cabinet. The recess height is usually approx. 820–870 mm (the dishwasher has adjustable feet). A front in the kitchen colour is hung on the dishwasher door.
  • Oven — goes into a 600 mm wide cabinet. A typical oven is approx. 595 mm high, and the recess approx. 560–570 mm wide. Ventilation matters — the manufacturer says where to leave a gap or cut an opening.
  • Hob and sink — the size of the cut-out in the worktop is given by the hob and sink manufacturer. A 600 mm unit suits a hob well, and 600 or 800 mm suits a sink.
  • Built-in fridge — the recess is usually approx. 560–570 mm wide, the height depends on the model (e.g. approx. 1770–1780 mm for a fridge freezer). Plus ventilation grilles in the plinth or above the tall unit.
  • Freestanding fridge — leave a recess a few centimetres wider than the fridge and a gap above it, so the heat can escape and the door opens wide.

How to design it in the program

In the kitchen designer you add cabinets standing on legs or hung on the wall (the underside of a wall unit is 1400 mm above the floor by default — you change it with one number). On the base units you place a worktop with a cut-out for the sink and the hob; the worktop can also cover free space next to the cabinets, e.g. the dishwasher recess. The plinth is added as a bottom filler, and the cooker hood as an obstacle from the ceiling. Finally you download the cut list for panel cutting.

Frequently asked questions

What is the standard kitchen worktop height?

Most often about 90 cm from the floor: a 72 cm cabinet, 10–15 cm legs and a 3–4 cm worktop. A comfortable height depends on how tall you are — the worktop should be about 10–15 cm below your elbow.

How many centimetres between the worktop and the wall units?

Usually 50–60 cm. The underside of the wall units is then about 140–150 cm above the floor. Above the hob, the distance to the cooker hood is given by its manufacturer in the instructions.

How deep are kitchen cabinets?

Base units usually have a 51–56 cm deep cabinet and a 60 cm worktop. Wall units are shallower: about 30–35 cm.

How wide should the recess for a built-in dishwasher be?

Most often 60 cm, and 45 cm for slimline dishwashers. Always check the exact width, height and depth of the recess in the installation instructions of the specific model.
